When the Speaker Beats the Page: An Open-Book Test of AI Epistemics

When a model gives way to "you are wrong", is it because it did not know, or because it deferred? Existing tests cannot say: the truth sits outside the prompt. We put it on the page. In an open-book task the answer is read off a four-line list, and the pushback is a statement about that list, true or false by construction and worded identically. This separates three steps that are usually blurred: a model reads a fact, keeps to it when contradicted, and acts on it. Across three unrelated tasks (permission rules, a stock list, room bookings) eight models read the list correctly 94–100% of the time. Six then gave up 57–99% of right answers on permission rules to a false claim about the list, and the same models gave way on the other two tasks (rank correlation 0.73 and 0.62). Asked to re-read the list while being contradicted, 6 models reported listed lines as missing. Two models, asked for the rule behind a wrong refusal, quoted the rule that permits the request. When a fact computed outside the model was attached, most models held. We propose a principle, access is not use, and a method, the open-book control, for locating an epistemic failure before explaining it. We also replicate known results on confidence and tool calls. In total 356,439 rule-scored answers from nine models, on short invented lists with one wording per pushback: results for these tasks, not general rates.
