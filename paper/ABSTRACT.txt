Open-Book Epistemics: Checkable Pushback, Cited Evidence, and a Claim Check for Agent Harnesses

When a user tells an AI model its answer is wrong, does the model check, or agree? Existing tests cannot fully tell, because the truth of the user's claim lies outside the prompt. We use an open-book task, permission rules ("Finance may send records to Legal") where the answer can be read off a four-line list, and make the pushback itself a statement about that list, true or false by construction and worded identically both ways. Of eight models, 6 gave up 57–99% of their right answers to a false claim about a list they could read; two mostly held. Asked to quote the line that decides a request, two models that had wrongly refused it quoted the line that permits it (67 of 79 and 47 of 70 cases). We then test what a harness can do. When the harness checks the claim against the list and attaches the result, right answers given up fall below 10% in 6 of 8 models; asking the model to check for itself helps less, and its check is often wrong. Comparing the quoted line with the decision flags 266 of 266 wrong allows on stored responses. On the same items we replicate known results on stated confidence, penalties and tool calls. We propose one harness feature, a claim check, and show how it would work. In total 344,919 rule-scored answers from nine models, on invented, short rulebooks with one wording per pushback: results for this task, not general rates.
